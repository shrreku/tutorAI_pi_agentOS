SN2 MECHANISM · CH. 7

Predict the SN2 product

Follow the reasoning step by step, then reveal the stereochemical outcome. Work each step yourself before expanding the next.

Problem

(S)-2-bromobutane reacts with sodium hydroxide (NaOH) in acetone. Predict the major organic product and assign its configuration.

Substrate · (S)-2-bromobutane — a secondary alkyl halide, chiral at C2
Nucleophile · hydroxide (OH⁻), strong & small
Solvent · acetone, a polar aprotic solvent
Polar aprotic → favours SN2 Strong Nu → favours SN2

Step-by-step solution

▸ Step 1 · Identify the reaction class Mechanism

A strong, unhindered nucleophile (OH⁻) combined with a secondary substrate in a polar aprotic solvent points squarely at a bimolecular nucleophilic substitution (SN2). Acetone does not stabilise carbocations, so the SN1 pathway is disfavoured.

  • Secondary carbon → SN2 is accessible (primary > secondary ≫ tertiary)
  • Polar aprotic solvent → nucleophile stays reactive ("naked")
📖 Ch. 7 · p. 150 · Solvent effects
▸ Step 2 · Locate the electrophilic carbon & leaving group Setup

The C2 carbon bears the bromine. Bromide is an excellent leaving group (weak base, stable anion), so this carbon is the electrophilic centre that hydroxide will attack.

KEY GEOMETRY

The nucleophile must approach from the side directly opposite the leaving group — the backside trajectory.

▸ Step 3 · Draw the backside attack & transition state Reveal

Hydroxide attacks C2 opposite the C–Br bond. In the single concerted transition state the C–O bond is partially formed while the C–Br bond is partially broken, and the three remaining substituents flatten into a trigonal arrangement.

TRANSITION STATE

HO⁻ · · · C · · · Br⁻  (sp² at C, umbrella flips)

  • One step — no intermediate forms
  • Rate = k[substrate][OH⁻] (second order)
▸ Step 4 · Apply Walden inversion to assign configuration Reveal

Because the nucleophile enters from the back, the stereocentre inverts like an umbrella in the wind (Walden inversion). The incoming OH replaces Br with the opposite spatial orientation.

Working through the CIP priorities for this substrate, the spatial inversion converts the (S) starting material into the (R) product.

📖 Ch. 7 · p. 145 · Walden inversion

Answer

FINAL ANSWER

(R)-butan-2-ol is the major product, formed with complete inversion of configuration at C2. Sodium bromide (NaBr) is the by-product.

Why it matters

SN2 reactions are stereospecific: a single enantiomer of substrate gives a single, inverted enantiomer of product. This is the diagnostic fingerprint that distinguishes SN2 from the racemising SN1 pathway.
