Nucleophilic substitution

Nucleophilic substitution is a fundamental class of reactions in organic chemistry in which an electron-rich nucleophile selectively bonds with the positive charge of an electrophilic carbon atom, displacing a leaving group.

Definition

The two main mechanisms are SN1 (unimolecular, via carbocation) and SN2 (bimolecular, concerted backside attack).

Intuition

Think of an umbrella flipping inside-out in a gust of wind — that's the stereochemical inversion during an SN2 backside attack.
