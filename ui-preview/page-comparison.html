SN1 vs SN2

Side-by-side breakdown of the two nucleophilic substitution mechanisms · Ch. 7

Overview

Both SN1 and SN2 swap a leaving group for a nucleophile, but they differ fundamentally in how the bonds break and form. SN2 is a single concerted step where the nucleophile attacks as the leaving group departs, so the rate depends on both species. SN1 proceeds through a discrete carbocation intermediate, so the rate depends only on the substrate. Reading the table row by row is the fastest way to predict which pathway dominates for a given substrate, nucleophile, and solvent.

Mechanism comparison

Property SN1 SN2
Kinetics First-order · rate = k[substrate] Second-order · rate = k[substrate][Nu]
Mechanism Two steps via a carbocation intermediate Single concerted step (no intermediate)
Substrate Tertiary > secondary (stable carbocation) Primary > secondary (low steric hindrance)
Nucleophile Weak nucleophiles are fine (not rate-limiting) Strong nucleophiles required
Solvent Polar protic (stabilises the carbocation) Polar aprotic (leaves Nu reactive)
Stereochemistry Racemisation (planar carbocation) Inversion · Walden inversion
Rate-determining step Carbocation formation (ionisation) The single concerted attack
When each dominates

SN2 wins for primary (and unhindered secondary) substrates with a strong nucleophile in a polar aprotic solvent — expect clean inversion of configuration. SN1 takes over for tertiary substrates, weak nucleophiles, and polar protic solvents — expect a racemic mixture from the planar carbocation. Secondary substrates sit in the contested middle, where solvent and nucleophile strength decide the outcome.
