Page 142 · 7.3 The SN2 Mechanism

The SN2 mechanism proceeds through a single concerted transition state in which the nucleophile approaches the substrate carbon from the side opposite the leaving group. As the new C–Nu bond forms, the C–L bond breaks simultaneously.

The geometry of the transition state is trigonal bipyramidal around the reacting carbon, with three substituents lying in a plane and the nucleophile and leaving group occupying the apical positions.

Highlighted · cited in tutor

Primary substrates react fastest because steric hindrance is minimized at the transition state.

Consequently, reaction rates follow the order methyl > 1° > 2° >> 3°, and tertiary substrates typically undergo elimination rather than substitution under these conditions.
