Chapter 7 · Nucleophilic substitution

Page 142 — Backside attack & substrate reactivity

Cited here

The bimolecular nucleophilic substitution (SN2) proceeds through a single, concerted transition state. The nucleophile approaches the electrophilic carbon along the axis directly opposite the leaving group, so that bond formation and bond breaking occur simultaneously.

⬩ CITED PASSAGE · p. 142

"Primary substrates react fastest in SN2 reactions because steric hindrance around the reacting carbon is minimised at the transition state. As substitution increases from primary to tertiary, the rate falls sharply and the SN1 pathway begins to dominate."

Because the nucleophile and the leaving group sit on opposite faces of the carbon during the transition state, the stereochemical configuration is inverted — the Walden inversion. This geometric requirement is why bulky substrates are so strongly disfavoured.

  • Rate law is second order: rate = k[substrate][nucleophile]
  • Reactivity order: methyl > 1° > 2° >> 3°
  • Polar aprotic solvents enhance nucleophile strength
