Nucleophilic substitution

8 concepts linked · Evidence available

Overview

Nucleophilic substitution is a class of organic reactions in which a nucleophile displaces a leaving group from an electrophilic carbon. The two limiting mechanisms — SN1 and SN2 — differ in molecularity, stereochemistry, and rate law.

Intuition

Think of an umbrella flipping inside-out in a gust of wind — that's the stereochemical inversion during an SN2 backside attack.
