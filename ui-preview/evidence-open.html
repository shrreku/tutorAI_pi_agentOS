SN2 mechanism

Backside attack, stereochemistry inversion, and the substrate factors that drive the concerted step.

Definition

The SN2 reaction is a one-step, bimolecular nucleophilic substitution in which the nucleophile attacks the electrophilic carbon from the side opposite the leaving group, displacing it in a single concerted process.

Because bond formation and bond breaking happen simultaneously, the rate depends on both substrate and nucleophile concentration — it is second order overall. 📖 Ch. 7 · p. 142

Intuition

Think of an umbrella flipping inside-out in a gust of wind — the stereochemistry at the carbon inverts (Walden inversion) as the nucleophile pushes the leaving group out the back.

Key factors

  • Primary substrates favor SN2 · steric hindrance at tertiary centres blocks backside attack
  • Strong, less-hindered nucleophiles accelerate the bimolecular step
  • Polar aprotic solvents keep the nucleophile reactive and "naked"
  • Good leaving groups stabilize the departing anion
