Rate laws, integrated kinetics, and the constants you need for SN1/SN2 problems · Ch. 7

Every formula below maps directly to a row in the rate analysis you ran for the SN2 mechanism. Use the Rate laws block to set up an expression, the Integrated kinetics block to solve for time or concentration, and the Temperature & energetics block when a problem gives you an activation energy.

Rate laws

Quantity Formula Notes
SN2 rate (bimolecular) rate = k[substrate][Nu] Second-order overall; first-order in each reactant
SN1 rate (unimolecular) rate = k[substrate] First-order; depends only on the substrate
Overall order n = m + p Sum of exponents in rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ᵖ
Rate constant units k → M⁽¹⁻ⁿ⁾·s⁻¹ Vary with overall order n (s⁻¹ for 1st, M⁻¹s⁻¹ for 2nd)

Integrated kinetics

Quantity Formula Notes
First-order decay [A] = [A]₀·e⁻ᵏᵗ Linear plot of ln[A] vs t, slope = −k
First-order half-life t½ = ln 2 / k Independent of starting concentration [A]₀
Second-order 1/[A] = 1/[A]₀ + kt Linear plot of 1/[A] vs t, slope = k
Second-order half-life t½ = 1 / (k[A]₀) Lengthens as the reaction proceeds

Temperature & energetics

Quantity Formula Notes
Arrhenius equation k = A·e⁻ᴱᵃ/ᴿᵀ A = pre-exponential factor; Eₐ = activation energy
Arrhenius (linear) ln k = ln A − Eₐ/RT Plot ln k vs 1/T, slope = −Eₐ/R
Two-temperature form ln(k₂/k₁) = −Eₐ/R·(1/T₂ − 1/T₁) Solve for Eₐ from two rate constants
Gibbs free energy ΔG‡ = ΔH‡ − TΔS‡ Activation free energy of the transition state
Constants & reminders
  • Gas constant R = 8.314 J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹
  • Avogadro Nₐ = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹
  • Convert temperature to Kelvin: T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15
  • ln 2 ≈ 0.693 — handy for half-life calculations
