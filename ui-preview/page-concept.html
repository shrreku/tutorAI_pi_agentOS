SN2 mechanism

Backside attack, stereochemistry inversion, and substrate factors.

Definition

The SN2 reaction is a one-step, bimolecular nucleophilic substitution in which the nucleophile attacks the electrophilic carbon from the side opposite the leaving group, displacing it in a concerted process.

Intuition

Think of an umbrella flipping inside-out in a gust of wind — the stereochemistry at the carbon inverts (Walden inversion).

Key factors

  • Primary substrates favor SN2 · steric hindrance slows the reaction
  • Strong nucleophiles accelerate the bimolecular step
  • Polar aprotic solvents keep the nucleophile reactive
  • Good leaving groups stabilize the departing anion
